    03 · Neer, the on-board assistant

    The filter that tells you what it knows.

    Neer (நீர், "water") shows a face and one short line. Every line comes from something it measured. When it isn't sure, it says so. It never says the water is "safe".

    • It explains itself. Not just a red light: what it saw, and what to do.
    • It forecasts. "About 40 days left" from how the filter is actually wearing, not a fixed timer.
    • It knows its limits. Doubtful data turns the light amber, never green.
    • It doesn't do the filtering. Gravity does. Neer watches, explains and locks.

    04 · What we will and won't claim

    Every claim waits for a lab result.

    After testing, we'll report

    • Bacteria reduction, with the exact number an NABL-accredited lab measures (E. coli and total coliform), not a hoped-for one.
    • Litres treated and filter life, from measured data.
    • That filtering needs no power, and the electronics fail closed.
    • Each stress test we actually ran, on film.

    We won't claim

    • Virus removal, until it is tested.
    • Removing dissolved salts, arsenic or fluoride. These filters don't.
    • That Neer detects germs. No low-cost real-time germ sensor exists.
    • "Safe", "pure", or "withstands anything".

    05 · Where it stands

    Building now. Results in November.

    AquaNXT is a work in progress. Designs are done in CAD; the prototypes, the Station's 30-day biolayer and the lab tests are under way for the ASU Venture X final.
